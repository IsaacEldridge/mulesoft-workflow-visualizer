## **Glossary of terms**  {#glossary-of-terms}

Here are a few terms you’ll see throughout this training doc and what they mean. Some terms are the general definitions while others are specific to the MuleSoft blog. 

**Web content writing** specifically means content that is relevant to a business’ goals, needs, and audience. Web content should contain keywords that aim to improve the business’ web presence (searchability) and SEO. Web content is heavily structured for search engines to improve things like organic search metrics. This differs from writing you might see in a book, newspaper, magazine, or even in a personal blog (separate from a company’s website). 

**Content,** when used in context of web content creation, is information a brand or person provides on a website or other electronic medium, e.g. social media platforms. 

**Search engine optimization (SEO)** is the process of refining and improving a website’s searchability and online presence through properly formatted section headers, keywords that are relevant to the content, alt-text on images, and hyperlinks to reputable source material. 

**SEO-focused content** means written content that primarily focuses on keywords and phrases relevant to the business and how these keywords can be used to drive organic traffic (unpaid, non-ad-based traffic) to a business website. These articles are often very structured with section headers that contain specific keywords that are relevant to the primary keyword (topic) at hand. 

**Optimization:** Whenever you hear content marketing teams talking about “optimization,” it usually means how to enhance or improve the readability and searchability of content online. 

**Keyword:** A keyword is the main idea, word, or phrase that an article or webpage is covering. Keywords are what people type into search engines to find results for their questions. For our context, keywords should be thought of as “main ideas” or core topics and speaking points for articles. 

**Short-tail keywords** are key phrases that are one or two words in length. E.g. “API management”

**Long-tail keywords** are key phrases that are three or more words in length and are usually full questions or queries. E.g. “What does API management for small businesses look like?”

**Headers,** when referring to SEO-focused content, are the section headers that precede paragraphs in a blog. These can be thought of as the **main ideas** or **main speaking points** of the article, and the larger the header number (H2 vs. H3 vs. H4), the more “important” the idea. 

**Alt text:** Short for “alternative text,” this is a written description of an image. In the instance an image cannot be viewed, the text-based description suffices as an explanation for what the image contents are. 

A **competitive analysis** of content is a structured analysis of competing content on the web. Competitive analyses are done by searching for a specific keyword via a search engine and seeing what relevant content comes up as recommended. This information is then used to craft an improved version of an outline for a blog post with relevant short- and long-tail keywords, phrases, and questions that users are searching for. 

## **Voice and tone guidelines** {#voice-and-tone-guidelines}

Why does aligning on voice and tone matter? Aligning blog content with standard voice and tone guidelines allows us to maintain cohesive messaging, build relationships with our readers through consistency, and provide better experiences for our audience. 

* **What is voice?** Our voice is our brand’s personality that we infuse into all communications. Our voice is consistent but it can evolve over time.   
* **What is tone?** Our tone is our brand’s current mood which adapts based on the situation, channel (e.g. email vs. article), or audience (e.g. developer vs. CIO) that we are using.  
* **What’s the difference?** Our voice reflects our *personality*; our tone is our *mood*.

### **Voice guidelines**  {#voice-guidelines}

We describe MuleSoft’s voice as human, clear, and inspiring. 

**Human:** Our voice is plain-spoken. We are just as comfortable speaking in the boardroom as we are speaking to developers. We write just as we speak, naturally and in a way that is tailored to the audience we are speaking to. We avoid unnecessary superlatives and cliches that make our voice sound dull. We turn to buzzwords, technical terminology, and industry lingo solely when it is the only way to meet readers where they are.

**Clear:** Our voice is active and simple. We write for scanning first, reading second – using as few words as possible and avoiding complex sentence structures that detract from effective communication. We choose our words carefully and use language that is actionable and guides readers every step of the way, making it easy to understand what we are saying and what they should do next. 

**Inspiring:** Our voice is energizing and urgent. We choose positive language, focusing on the limitless possibilities we open up for our customers by writing in a way that allows them to dream big. We use persuasive language that inspires readers and demonstrates we are mission-critical by answering why us, why now, and most importantly, *how*. Although we seek to inspire readers and foster urgency, we don’t like to exaggerate or patronize. 

* **Add your own flair:** Include your personality in your writing, but make sure that any claims are proven with data, research, case studies, and/or customer experiences, stories, and testimonials that can be linked within your content.    
* **Write in an active voice with second-person POV:** Please write in an active voice and from a second-person POV (you/yours/ours), not first-person (I/me/mine). When you write in a second-person POV and in an active voice, your article becomes more dynamic and engaging to read.   
  * [Active vs. passive voice](https://www.grammarly.com/blog/active-vs-passive-voice/): “Active voice means that a sentence has a subject that acts upon its verb. Passive voice means that a subject is a recipient of a verb’s action.”   
    * **Active voice example:** Teams use MuleSoft Composer to connect apps and go digital faster.  
    * **Passive voice example:** MuleSoft Composer is used by teams to connect apps and go digital faster.

### **Tone guidelines**  {#tone-guidelines}

Our voice is *what* we say and our tone is *how* we say it. Our voice reflects our personality, and our tone is our mood. For this reason, our tone changes based on the variables like the audience, medium, and situation. Our blog tone is a combination of casual \+ professional (human), approachable (clear), and innovative (inspiring). 

## **Editorial guidelines** {#editorial-guidelines}

* **Blog and content objectives:** The MuleSoft blog drives awareness, education, and engagement with prospects and customers. Are you trying to drive awareness, education, or engagement with prospects or customers? If not, your article is not suited for the MuleSoft blog and may be a better fit elsewhere.   
* **Originality:** Is the article an original piece of content that is relevant to our audience? We don’t accept syndicated content that has been previously published elsewhere on any other blog (including Medium) or social channel (i.e. LinkedIn article). In the off-chance your proposal was published on an internal site (meaning only employees can access and read it), we will allow the content to be submitted as a net-new proposal.  
* **AI-generated content:** While we welcome the use of AI to *ideate* content, we cannot publish blogs that are entirely AI-generated. If we suspect AI was used to fully write content, we will ask you to either rewrite your blog or cancel the publication. AI-generated content often has very obvious, common words, formatting, and structure, and it is easy to tell when a contributor submits a fully AI-written draft. We want *your* ideas, *your* voice – not Gemini, ChatGPT, Claude, Grok, etc. If you have questions about this, don’t hesitate to reach out. AI is useful when used as a stepping stone for ideas, *not* as a crutch for creativity. 

**Style and language:** 

* We follow the AP Stylebook (but we use the Oxford/serial comma\!) and we write in American English.   
* Keep your article conversational, but make sure to include legitimate source material to enhance the reliability of your content.    
* Write from a second-person point of view (you, your, yours – not “I, me, mine”) with an active voice.    
* Ensure your article has a common narrative thread and tells a story. Any personal experience or customer story must have data, external source material, and factual evidence to support the message. We do not want opinion-based content that sounds like a personal blog; we want content that is informative, valuable, and relatable, not self-serving or braggadocious. 

**Write a good headline:** The headline is the first (and sometimes only) thing your target audience sees, so it’s critical that it draws the reader in and excites them to read the article. 

* All headlines on the blog must be 60 characters or fewer for search optimization. Anything beyond 60 characters will be cut off by search engines, so we will edit and shorten titles that exceed this length.    
* Include the primary SEO keyword near the beginning of the title.   
* Set clear expectations for your reader. What are they going to get out of reading your article? Make that value clear in the headline, and avoid “clickbait” headlines.

**Formatting:**

* Use our proprietary Google Doc template to draft your proposal and blog. We do not accept Quip, Word documents, Pages, PDFs, or content outside of our template. You will have to change your content into Google Doc format to qualify for publication.   
* **Formatting titles and subtitles:** We use title case for the blog title, but sentence case for headlines. Sentence case means only capitalizing the first word and any proper nouns.  

**Citations, references, and backlinking:**

* ​​We aim to have relevant keywords backlinked (a form of hyperlinking) in each article.   
  * Do not hyperlink full sentences. A hyperlink should contain a keyword or a key phrase, typically no longer than five words long.   
* All claims, statements of truth, assumptions, data, research, numbers, facts, figures, etc. must include direct source links to the referenced material. If facts or statements of truth are present without a source, they will be reworded or removed so that we can maintain editorial integrity across the blog.   
  * Examples of statements of truth include “All people believe X,” “Most people believe X,” or “Everyone knows that X.”  
* If you are linking to an external site, please use sources that are informative instead of promotional. Driving to external brands’ homepages (instead of a specific article, white paper, press release, etc.) is not allowed.  
* Please ensure any external link **does not come from or mention the following list of competitors:** Amazon, Axway, Boomi, CA Tech, Google (Apigee), IBM, Informatica, Jitterbit, Kong, Microsoft, Oracle, Red Hat (incl. 3scale), SAP, SnapLogic, Software AG, Talend, TIBCO, Workato, UiPath, AutomationAnywhere.

**General media guidelines:**

**Article featured image guidelines:**

* The blog team does not create custom images for articles. 

**In-text image guidelines:**  

* You MUST have a Google Drive link with all images you include in your article draft. This is mandatory. Images must be labeled in the order they appear in the article. It doesn’t matter if these images are screenshots from a slide deck or product page or images from an external source. We must have a folder with the raw image files.   
* You must include image captions. See accessibility guidelines for more information. 

## **Accessibility guidelines** {#accessibility-guidelines}

**Scannability and reading level:** Make sure your article is easily scannable by reducing the length of your sentences (25 words or fewer) and paragraphs (3 to 4 sentences per paragraph) and adding headings and quotes throughout your post. Your article should be written at an 8th or 9th-grade reading level so it is easy to understand. Check your reading level via [Hemingwayapp.com](http://hemingwayapp.com/).

* Avoid making up new acronyms and abbreviations and only use those that are widely known. If you do need to use any, explain the abbreviations at the beginning of the article for those who aren’t familiar with the abbreviations. 

**Image captions:** If you include an image in your article, it must have a descriptive image caption that can be used as alt-text describing the image to screen reading software for those who have low vision. Keep the image caption under 125 characters, and don’t include any promotional language (i.e. a CTA) in image captions. 

**Videos:** All videos on the blog are automatically set to “press to play” versus auto-start. All videos must be uploaded to Vidyard to be included in the blog. ***You are responsible for uploading video content to Vidyard***. If you fail to upload your video so that it can be embedded, it will instead be added as a clickable link, not a playable widget. 

**Link accessibility:** Avoid non-descriptive link CTAs like “click here” and instead describe the links so readers know where they’ll be taken. A good example of accessible linking is, “Learn more about the state of digital transformation in our [Connectivity Benchmark Report](https://www.mulesoft.com/lp/reports/connectivity-benchmark).”

## **Content marketing process and guidelines FAQs** {#content-marketing-process-and-guidelines-faqs}

**Who can submit content? Is it just employees?** As of FY26, internal employees are our primary contributors. However, MuleSoft Community Ambassadors (in collaboration with the Community team) are allowed to write for us as well. Additionally, on a case-by-case basis, partners or customers may be eligible to contribute. 

**Can I submit multiple article requests at one time?** No. If you have an active request out with us, you must complete the full cycle (i.e. submission, approval, publication, and promotion before submitting a second request). We want to diversify our contributor pool, and it’s not fair to have the same voices submitting content back-to-back without giving others a chance to shine as well. The only time we have the same authors’ voices featured back-to-back is when our PM team develops content to inform readers of new products, features, or news.  

**Note:** If you have an active request in progress and submit a second proposal, it will not be attended to until your initial content request cycle is complete. There will also be intentional buffer time between your first published request and any subsequent requests so that no two articles by the same author are published back-to-back. Additionally, we will do our best to ensure each author's byline (except for PMMs) is only on the blog once monthly.  

**What if I want to submit a series of articles?** We do not publish article series and have not since 2022\. If you have a relevant topic that gets approved, you are welcome to write a longer article with the content you’d intend for a multi-part series. 

**Is there a specific word count I have to reach?** We prefer mid-length (1000–1200) articles, but are open to articles that exceed 2000–3000 words. Anything beyond this range may be edited for concision or split into multiple articles. 

**I’m submitting on someone else’s behalf (e.g. my manager, my direct report, an SME, etc.), how do I submit the content request form?** Submit the form as if you are submitting as them. In the draft template, include their name, email address, headshot, and intended author’s bio. This information is important to get the correct byline for publication. 

**When will my article go live?** After submitting your topic and getting it approved, editing, any rewriting, and final review from the blog team and any necessary stakeholders, articles usually go live within 4–6 weeks of initial draft submission. However, **this time frame is subject to change** based on the blog team’s editorial calendar space, and/or events, conferences, and product launch timelines. 

**Note:** We usually publish 3 to 5 articles per week, and our editorial calendar places priority on business-critical content, e.g. product launches, general news, and event information. If we already have a content-heavy week, your article will be moved to the next best or free slot as determined by our team. We appreciate your patience.
